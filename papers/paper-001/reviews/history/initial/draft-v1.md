# Detecting and Reducing Hallucinations in Large Language Models: A Review of Verification Techniques

## Introduction

Evaluating a generated statement requires deciding what counts as evidence for it. SelfCheckGPT compares sampled responses without an external factual database (Manakul et al., 2023); Chain-of-Verification separates checking questions from an initial response (Dhuliawala et al., 2024); FActScore assesses atomic claims against a specified knowledge source (Min et al., 2023). These approaches make different judgments: agreement among outputs, answers to verification questions, and documentary support. This review compares their benefits and limitations, asking whether improvements in particular evaluations justify confidence in factual truth. It examines their mechanisms, reported outcomes, and methodological constraints rather than ranking incompatible scores. Consistency checks, separated verification, and documentary fact-checking offer distinct benefits, but each provides fallible evidence rather than guarantees of factual truth.

## Consistency and Separated Checking

Consistency can support useful detection while remaining vulnerable to repeated mistakes. On WikiBio-generated biographies, SelfCheckGPT's Prompt variant obtained nonfactual-detection AUC-PR of 93.42, versus 83.21 for average negative log probability, on the reported 0–100 scale (Manakul et al., 2023). The main Prompt experiment used ChatGPT to evaluate 20 GPT-3 samples, so it was not purely the generator evaluating itself. These ranking measures are not accuracy percentages. Conversely, Tan et al. (2025) constructed question-answering subsets containing errors repeated across 15 stochastic samples. Semantic-entropy detection achieved AUROC ranging from 0.3633 to 0.5427 on consistent-error subsets across four models and two tasks. This does not refute SelfCheckGPT's result: the detectors, datasets, and metrics differ. It instead exposes a boundary of consistency as evidence. Stable outputs can be wrong, and performance on constructed error subsets cannot establish how frequently such errors occur in deployment.

Separating checks can improve results without establishing independent knowledge. Chain-of-Verification uses the same model to draft, formulate questions, answer them, and revise; its factored variant answers questions in separate contexts without the initial response (Dhuliawala et al., 2024). On 418 closed-book MultiSpanQA factoid questions, factored Llama 65B improved F1 from the few-shot baseline's 0.39 to 0.48. Context separation is therefore a useful intervention in that setting, but not independently sourced evidence. SAC³ combines semantically equivalent question variants with responses from target and verifier models; its best configuration differs between the two generated-answer datasets (Zhang et al., 2023). Moreover, Tan et al. (2025) found overlapping repeated errors between models. Consulting another model consequently does not guarantee correctness, while different answers do not establish statistical independence. These findings support specifying what is separated before describing a verification procedure as independent.

## Documentary Fact-Checking

Documentary checking changes the evidential basis by connecting claims to inspectable material. FActScore measures the proportion of atomic facts supported by a knowledge source, and its automated estimators include retrieval from Wikipedia (Min et al., 2023). With human-produced atomic facts supplied as an oracle, an instruction-tuned LLaMA evaluator achieved Not-supported micro-F1 of 78.7 using retrieval, versus 66.0 using the study's self-check method on InstructGPT outputs. That comparison supports retrieval under the stated conditions, not universal superiority or end-to-end decomposition accuracy. Similarly, replacing SelfCheckGPT's samples with an available reference paragraph improved its reported measures (Manakul et al., 2023). However, that ablation bypassed the problem of finding suitable evidence. Neither experiment establishes that a noisy retrieval pipeline will reproduce the advantages of already available source material.

Traceability also differs from truth. RARR retrieves evidence and revises generated passages, improving human-rated attribution in its three main evaluation settings (Gao et al., 2023). Yet its task does not evaluate source trustworthiness or explicitly resolve conflicting evidence. Improved attribution therefore cannot automatically be interpreted as reduced objective falsehood. VeriScore further distinguishes contradiction from inconclusive evidence, although both can become “unsupported” in binary evaluation (Song et al., 2024). Its qualitative inspection of 80 claims illustrates how snippets can lack information needed to assess complex relations. Because that sample deliberately balanced supported and unsupported claims, it is not a prevalence estimate. Together, these studies indicate that documentary checking supplies an inspectable basis for judgment while leaving source quality, completeness, and interpretation consequential. Failure to retrieve support must remain distinguishable from evidence of falsity.

## Critical Comparison

Apparently opposing findings often concern different interventions. CoVe's targeted factual-question procedure improved performance, whereas Huang et al. (2024) found lower final reasoning scores after two rounds of intrinsic answer-feedback revision in the reported GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 conditions. Those results do not constitute a controlled contradiction: targeted checking and general reconsideration differ, as do tasks and models (Dhuliawala et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2024). Nor is reasoning accuracy interchangeable with open-ended factuality. The distinction between assessment and improvement also applies to external evidence. Feeding RARR-revised passages back to the model improved Natural Questions short answers but not StrategyQA answers (Gao et al., 2023). Detecting unsupported content, improving attribution, and repairing the reasoning needed for a correct answer are separate accomplishments.

Improved scores must also be interpreted alongside retained information. On Wikidata, two-step CoVe increased precision from 0.17 to 0.36 while mean correct entities fell from 0.59 to 0.38 (Dhuliawala et al., 2024). In RAGTruth's Llama-2-7B/Mistral-7B selection experiment, zero-span filtering reduced labeled hallucination from 52.4% among 450 randomly selected responses to 19.3% among 328 retained responses (Niu et al., 2024). This measures context faithfulness; rejected responses are not corrected successes. FActScore accordingly recommends reporting abstention and fact counts alongside precision (Min et al., 2023). Nevertheless, gains need not merely reflect deletion: CoVe improved both precision and recall on its MultiSpanQA subset (Dhuliawala et al., 2024). The appropriate comparison must therefore preserve denominators and distinguish useful retained content from conditional accuracy.

## Limitations and Research Needs

Study designs constrain the strength of broader conclusions. SelfCheckGPT sampled from the longest 20% of WikiBio articles and double-annotated only 201 of 1,908 sentences, limiting both domain coverage and the scope of its annotation-agreement assessment (Manakul et al., 2023). RARR's main experiments used single runs, with one rater per example after its pilot; its repeated-run check concerned one automatic evaluation condition (Gao et al., 2023). VeriScore selected a fine-tuned checkpoint after evaluating both synthetic test data and human-annotated cases (Song et al., 2024). Consequently, the human set should not simply be assumed to provide untouched final evaluation, although this does not quantify any resulting bias. SAC³'s simplified model-call accounting likewise cannot establish measured latency or monetary cost (Zhang et al., 2023). These limitations do not erase reported benefits. They motivate matched comparisons with held-out human judgments, common outcomes, coverage measures, and actual resource costs. Evaluating source reliability and conflicting evidence would also strengthen documentary comparisons. This bounded review supports conditional conclusions, not an exhaustive ranking or deployment guarantee.

## Conclusion

The approaches provide different reasons for confidence: repeated-output stability, separately elicited model judgments, and traceable documentary support. Their benefits remain conditional on the checking procedure and evaluation setting. Repeated errors qualify consistency-based confidence, while attribution leaves questions of source reliability unresolved (Gao et al., 2023; Tan et al., 2025). Consistency checks, separated verification, and documentary fact-checking offer distinct benefits, but each provides fallible evidence rather than guarantees of factual truth. Responsible comparison therefore requires identifying what was checked, against which evidence, and with what loss of useful information. The reviewed results justify qualified use of verification, without establishing a universally superior method or optimal combined system.
