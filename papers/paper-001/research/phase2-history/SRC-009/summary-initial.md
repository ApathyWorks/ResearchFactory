## 2. Research Overview

RARR is a post-generation procedure that retrieves evidence and revises text to improve its attribution while retaining its original intent and wording. Attribution means support from retrieved material; it does not establish the truth or trustworthiness of that material (C001). Question generation, retrieval, agreement checking and limited revision implement this objective (C002). The experiments measure both evidence support and preservation, because a revision can improve one while substantially degrading the other (C003–C007).

## 3. Research Questions

The explicit task objective is to produce a revised passage and supporting evidence report that balance attribution and preservation (C001). As an inferred research question, the experiments ask whether a prompted research-and-revise pipeline can achieve that balance across factoid answers, reasoning chains and conversational answers (C002–C005). The ablations investigate query formulation and agreement checking (C008–C009). These are objectives and experimental comparisons, not preregistered hypotheses.

## 4. Methodology

- **Design and pipeline:** The main RARR implementation prompts PaLM 540B for query generation, agreement checking and editing. It samples question generation three times, retrieves five Google results per question, ranks four-sentence evidence windows and keeps one snippet per query. The revision model edits sequentially only when disagreement is detected; an attribution report contains at most five snippets (C002).
- **Evaluation material:** Each generation-model/dataset combination has 150 development and 150 test passages. NQ uses development questions, StrategyQA training questions, and QReCC development conversations. Main results concern PaLM answers on NQ/StrategyQA and LaMDA answers on QReCC; additional GPT-3 generations are separate appendix experiments (C004).
- **Comparators:** Fully supervised EFEC receives evidence from RARR's research stage; LaMDA Research performs its research/revision stages on the supplied text. These adapted comparators were not optimized for the same preservation objective (C007).
- **Measures:** Human AIS scores the fraction of fully supported sentences. Preservation combines a binary intent judgment with normalized character-level Levenshtein similarity. Main F1AP combines human attribution and combined preservation; the ablations instead combine automatic attribution and Levenshtein preservation (C003). These two F1AP variants must not be treated as interchangeable.
- **Annotation and uncertainty:** After a three-rater pilot, each example received one rater. Main experiments use one run; a separate three-run NQ automatic evaluation estimates variability for that condition (C011). No universal claim of statistical significance is inferred here from the point estimates.

## 5. Key Findings

1. Human attribution improved across all three main settings while intent preservation remained 90.0–95.6%; exact task-specific results are in C005.
2. Higher attribution alone did not imply a better preservation tradeoff: on QReCC, EFEC obtained higher final AIS but much lower combined preservation than RARR (C006–C007).
3. Removing agreement checking reduced NQ preservation and automatic F1AP, supporting its role in limiting unnecessary edits under that condition (C008).
4. Generated questions were more robust than sentence queries to removal of Wikipedia in the reported NQ and StrategyQA experiment (C009).
5. Improved attribution did not consistently improve downstream answers: NQ answer accuracy improved, whereas all tested revision systems reduced StrategyQA accuracy (C010).
6. Results leave substantial uncertainty and domain limits: the main study uses single runs and single-rater judgments; exploratory SummEval attribution decreased, while ELI5 attribution changed little (C011–C012).
7. Attribution remains vulnerable to untrustworthy or conflicting evidence, and the model may preserve claims it cannot support (C001, C013).

## 7. Author Conclusions

The authors conclude that post-generation research and revision can improve evidence attribution while retaining much of the input's intent and form in their studied settings (C001–C005). They recognize substantial remaining difficulty, including dependence on retrieval, logical consistency and domain suitability (C010–C013). Their suggestions for prompt adaptation and smaller models are future-work proposals, not established remedies (C013).

## 8. Research Limitations

**AUTHOR_REPORTED_LIMITATIONS:** Evidence support cannot establish source truth, and conflicting evidence is not explicitly resolved (C001). Noisy retrieval and unchanged downstream reasoning can produce defective revisions (C010). The authors acknowledge limitations of their metrics, domain coverage, treatment of unattributed statements and computational expense (C013). Appendix tests include weak or negative attribution outcomes outside the main settings (C012).

**ADDITIONAL_OBSERVATIONS:** The single-run main evaluation and single-rater post-pilot annotation warrant caution about the stability of small differences (inference from C011). Systems' attribution reports differ, so attribution comparisons must retain their evidence-report conditions (C003, C005). High preservation is not necessarily desirable when the original argument is substantially wrong (interpretation grounded in C010, C013). These observations are not additional experiments or externally verified criticisms.
